UNDERGRADUATE THESIS · URP 4000 · 2026

Wavelet Decomposed Short Term Daily Precipitation Forecasting Using Artificial Neural Networks in Khulna, Bangladesh

A station-based framework for short-term rainfall forecasting, trend analysis and extreme precipitation assessment.

Study area Khulna District, Bangladesh
Observation period 1985–2019
Rain gauges 5 stations
Supervisor Dr. Tusar Kanti Roy

Research Overview

This undergraduate thesis develops short-term daily precipitation forecasting models for Khulna, Bangladesh. The study combines daily rain-gauge observations with meteorological information from MERRA-2 and uses wavelet-based signal decomposition and artificial neural networks to represent the nonlinear and variable behaviour of precipitation.

In addition to forecasting, the research examines long-term rainfall trends, temporal persistence and the return periods of extreme precipitation events.

Data & Study Design

  • Rainfall observations: Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD) daily precipitation data from 1985 to 2019.
  • Meteorological inputs: MERRA-2 reanalysis data.
  • Stations: Chalna, Dumuria, Kapilmuni, Khulna and Paikgacha.
  • Pre-processing: Missing-value treatment using MissForest, chronological sorting, lagged-feature construction and feature standardisation.

Methodology

The forecasting workflow combines wavelet decomposition with neural-network models and a stacked ensemble. Candidate networks include Radial Basis Function ANN (RBF-ANN), Wavelet-MLP (W-MLP), simulated-annealing-based MLP variants and a W-MLP / W-SAS-MLP stacked ensemble. Optuna is used for network tuning, while ARIMA provides a benchmark for comparison.

Key Findings

0.66–0.73 Reported KGE range across five stations
2.639–12.12 mm Reported MAE range across five stations
0.771–0.845 DFA α range, indicating persistent rainfall behaviour
  • Significant rainfall trends were reported at four of the five stations. Khulna station did not show a significant trend (Mann–Kendall p = 0.8647).
  • The study reports that the wavelet-neural-network and ensemble approaches outperformed the traditional ARIMA benchmark by a mean margin of 40.6%.
  • Duration-dependent rainfall intensities were higher for longer return periods: the presentation reports approximately 25–150 mm/day for 2-year events and 75–450 mm/day for 25-year events across durations and stations.

Planning Relevance

The thesis identifies potential applications for localized rainfall early-warning services, drainage and waterlogging management in Khulna, and agricultural planning. It recommends integrating forecast information with return-period analysis to support preparedness and operational decision-making.
