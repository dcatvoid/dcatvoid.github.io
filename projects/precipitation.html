Wavelet Decomposed Short Term Daily Precipitation Forecasting Using Artificial Neural Networks in Khulna, Bangladesh

A station-based framework for short-term rainfall forecasting, trend analysis and extreme precipitation assessment.

This undergraduate thesis develops short-term daily precipitation forecasting models for Khulna, Bangladesh. The study combines daily rain-gauge observations with meteorological information from MERRA-2 and uses wavelet-based signal decomposition and artificial neural networks to represent the nonlinear and variable behaviour of precipitation.

In addition to forecasting, the research examines long-term rainfall trends, temporal persistence and the return periods of extreme precipitation events.

  • Rainfall observations: BMD daily precipitation data, 1985–2019.
  • Meteorological inputs: MERRA-2 reanalysis.
  • Stations: Chalna, Dumuria, Kapilmuni, Khulna and Paikgacha.
  • Pre-processing: MissForest imputation, chronological sorting, lagged features, standardisation.
  • Forecast models: RBF-ANN, W-MLP, simulated-annealing MLP variants and a W-MLP / W-SAS-MLP stacked ensemble; Optuna tuning; ARIMA benchmark.
  • Trend & persistence: Sen’s slope, Mann–Kendall, DFA.
  • Extremes: Gumbel distribution and return periods.
  • Evaluation: KGE, R², MAE, RMSE.
  • Significant rainfall trends were reported at four of the five stations; Khulna station did not show a significant trend (Mann–Kendall p = 0.8647).
  • Wavelet-neural-network and ensemble approaches outperformed the ARIMA benchmark by a mean margin of 40.6%.
  • Approximately 25–150 mm/day for 2-year events and 75–450 mm/day for 25-year events across durations and stations.

The thesis identifies applications for localized rainfall early-warning services, drainage and waterlogging management in Khulna, and agricultural planning. It recommends integrating forecast information with return-period analysis to support preparedness and operational decision-making.
